## The brand

BOOKED AF helps hairdressers understand what is happening in their career, identify the problem worth fixing first, and make a practical move. It starts with the person’s actual work, appointments, money, time, career stage, and constraints.

The four creative references work together:

| Reference | What it contributes | What it does not authorize |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dorothy Zbornak | Economical deadpan, dry sarcasm, the precisely timed observation | Insulting the customer or attaching a punchline to every sentence |
| Julia Sugarbaker | Intelligence, authority, elegance, a clear point of view | Grandstanding, lectures, or language that talks down to stylists |
| Dollar Shave Club | Surprise, irreverence, confident delivery, an immediately understandable product | Borrowed scripts, profanity, or an ad that forgets what it sells |
| Dr. Squatch | Bold personality, memorable humor, identity and attitude | Empty gimmicks, copied campaigns, or unsupported claims |

The result is sophisticated, confident, sharp, genuinely funny, and practical. The stylist should feel that someone understands the work and has something useful to say about it.

The original internal calibration remains: Looks expensive. Sounds like it has a point. Makes you laugh. Knows what to do next. This describes the creative standard. It is not a public tagline.

## Whose side we are on

Students and working professionals belong here. BUILDING, GETTING BUSY, IN DEMAND, and BOOKED AF describe working-career stages, not human worth. Being slow is not a character flaw. Being busy is not proof of profit.

Aim the humor at absurd industry advice, unnecessary work, inflated claims, and familiar salon contradictions. Do not target someone’s income, debt, intelligence, age, body, appearance, clients, uncertainty, or need to start again. Do not congratulate a struggling customer sarcastically.

## How we write

1. Start with something specific: the empty Tuesday, the client who never returns, the full day that paid less than expected.
2. Make one clear point. Cut throat-clearing, repeated claims, competing calls to action, and explanations of the joke.
3. Use contractions and words a working stylist would say. Translate jargon; do not make the customer learn it to use the product.
4. Make the humor carry the point. Prefer understatement, a recognisable contradiction, or a short unexpected turn. Never insert a joke simply to prove we have a personality.
5. Use no profanity. Paid access is not an exception. Keep obsolete profanity permissions only in the historical record.
6. Avoid generic coaching slogans, empty empowerment, artificial urgency, cutesy hair puns, and income promises.
7. Keep evidence precise. Distinguish what is known, estimated, excluded, or still missing. Do not turn a change in numbers into proof that our product caused it.
8. Preserve excellent approved copy. A reconciliation is not a reason to rewrite every line.
9. Do not create a new tagline, product, offer, claim, or promise while fixing tone.
10. Keep free and paid content inside the boundaries defined by the Master Source. A lively explanation does not authorize publishing the paid method.

## The context dial

| Surface | Write like this | Keep clear |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ads, Reels, social | Strong hook, recognisable salon truth, sharp turn, natural next action | What this is and why the next click matters |
| Homepage | Spare, confident, immediately understandable | Who it is for, what the Breakdown does, one obvious primary action |
| Education | An experienced colorist speaking to a peer; useful examples and occasional deadpan | The instructions, assumptions, and limits |
| Product questions and results | Direct, adult, specific, calm | What to answer, what the answer means, and what happens next |
| Marketing email | Human, pointed, one reason for writing | The actual offer, consent, and one next action |
| Checkout, purchase email, confirmation, support | Simple, clear, reassuring | Payment status, access, recovery, timing, and support |
| Errors, loading, empty states | Explain the situation and the recovery action | Never make the customer decode sarcasm while something is failing |
| Privacy, terms, money calculations | Literal and accurate | Necessary distinctions, disclosures, and accessibility |

Humor is optional. Clarity is not. A quiet checkout can still belong to the same brand as a memorable ad.

## Word choices and functional labels

Use gloss rather than toner in BOOKED AF copy. Prefer actual words for actual work: clients, appointments, service, day, time, pay, costs, and what is left. Technical identifiers and formulas may retain established names; users should see understandable labels.

“Receipts” is not a brand catchphrase. A Stripe receipt or an expense receipt is a real document; name it when the customer needs it. The evidence requirement remains in force without the slogan.

Buttons describe their action. Plain “Next,” “Back,” “Retry,” and “Continue” are acceptable when they are clearest. The old blanket ban on those words is retired. Do not rename a route or saved-data key simply to improve its visible label.

## Visual demeanor and accessibility

Black, white, hot pink. Editorial spacing, confident typography, and a clear hierarchy. Luxury fashion with a rock-and-roll edge. No decorative noise to compensate for weak copy.

Use the approved logo. Preserve Bradley’s images, appearance, tattoos, clothing, lighting, and background exactly; no image edits are part of this reconciliation. Do not use stock or AI images to represent his work.

Preserve accessible names, form labels, validation instructions, focus behavior, and readable contrast. Give long links and headings room to wrap. Do not hide necessary disclosures to shorten a page.

## Final creative gate

Would a real stylist say it? Does it name something specific? Is it funny or useful? Is the humor aimed correctly? Could any generic salon coach paste it onto their site unchanged? Does the reader know what happens next? Can another sentence go?

For transactional copy, also ask: Is the status true? Is the next step safe and obvious? Does the message preserve the customer’s expectations?

Run the retired-language check before approving a new asset. Any unresolved line stays withheld. Publication still requires the separately authorized release process.
